# 02 — Chin Hin Group, researched

> Updates and extends `refdocs/context/04_RESEARCH.md` §A. Labels: ✅ primary source seen · 🟡 reputable secondary · 🔶 vendor/self-reported · ❓ inference or unverified. Researched 2026-10-08.

## 1. Who they are

Chin Hin Group Berhad (Bursa: CHINHIN, 5273) is a Malaysian integrated builder: building materials, construction engineering, property development and home & living, plus commercial vehicles. It started as a hardware business and calls its internal value chain the **IntraBuild ecosystem**: building materials feed the group's construction and property arms as well as outside customers ✅ (FY2025 annual report excerpt: "The Building Materials division anchors the Group's IntraBuild ecosystem"). It has about 3,000 employees and presence in 13 countries 🟡 (Digital News Asia, Nov 2025); group managing director is Datuk Wira Chiau Haw Choon. HQ is Menara Chin Hin, Sri Petaling, Kuala Lumpur ✅.

The brief's two customer types map onto this directly: **outside contractors/developers** and **the group's own construction and property businesses**.

Full-year FY2025: revenue >RM4bn (+25%), building materials RM1.90bn revenue / RM134.1m PBT ✅. Pipeline RM5.29bn = RM2.18bn unbilled property sales + RM1.81bn construction orders + RM1.28bn Signature International (SIB) backlog ✅.

**Reading it as a plant manager and as a CFO.** Volume is up, profit is thin. Building-materials PBT fell on "higher operating costs and tighter margins in a softer market" ✅. Management's stated near-term priorities include "enhancing productivity and operational efficiency across the building materials segment" and "ramping up new AAC capacity" ✅. That is our opening: *utilisation and margin per m³ are the lever, and the decision rule is how you pull it.* AAC + precast margin (≈10.8% PBT) is a little higher than ready-mix (≈10.3%); both are PBT margins, not contribution margins ❓.

## 4. Capacity — what is known and what is not

- Nameplate AAC: ~1.2M m³ → >2.2M m³ with the third plant (RM80m, Teeyer equipment, "world's largest single AAC production line") ✅.
- Timeline slipped from "full production by 31 May 2026" (MOU, Jun 2025) to "commissioning by July 2026" (Feb 2026 results) ✅. **I found no confirmation that it is operating** → PRD **OQ-15**. Do not state "2.2M m³ is running" in the pitch.
- Management has described being *capacity-constrained before* (full capacity, 4–5 month lead times in 2016–17 ✅🟡) and *under-utilised before* (about 30% at Kota Tinggi end-2019, per context `04`, not re-verified ❓). The cycle is the story; see 01 §2.
- Group eliminations of RM336.5m in 1H FY26 show how much trade flows between segments, but they cover all inter-segment trade (including property → construction), so they are **not** a measure of materials sold internally ❓. The context pack's "≈RM200m trade between building materials and construction (2024)" comes from a chairman interview 🟡 and is the better specific figure.

| Sales execs "can just ask the AI agents" across "10 business units and 10,000 products" for quotations | Digital News Asia | 🟡 |
| Pikom Digital Excellence Award 2025 (Outstanding Digital Transformation) | Microsoft story | 🔶 |
| Saw's phrases: "Whoever wins AI wins the whole cake"; "it's the faster defeating the slower"; "velocity decides"; "Human intelligence and artificial intelligence must work together" | Interviews/stories | 🟡 |

**What this means for the pitch (❓ my reading):**
- They want *velocity* and *business-led outcomes* ("purpose-built to drive practical, business-led outcomes"). Lead with the ringgit decision, then the agents.
- They are already buying an AI-enabled ERP. Plant Load Radar must be explicitly **ERP-agnostic and complementary**: capture messy orders that never reach the ERP (WhatsApp, paper), decide, then write structured results *to* the ERP when it is ready. Don't compete with the Kingdee quotation agent.
- Their stated AI ladder ends in agentic replacement of workflows. Our "AI decides allocation is premature" must be framed as a *stage gate* (earn autonomy with a ledger of approved decisions), not as reluctance. See 03 §5.
- They have already run a student hackathon with a *procurement planning agent* challenge (context `04`). Ours is about *allocation*, a different decision.

## 7. Risks to be aware of (public)

- Earnings pressure (net profit −64% in Q2) may make the CFO impatient with anything that isn't cash-visible ✅.
- Cost inflation across the sector: diesel +44% (Feb→Jul), ready-mix +15%, OPC +6.7% (MBAM/PKBM via FMT) 🟡; Rehda reports +13% construction cost (Mar–Jun) 🟡.
- Soft property market (Rehda) could slow internal call-offs even as Chin Hin's own unbilled sales (RM2.18bn) provide visibility ✅🟡.
