# 12 — Interview Prep

**Format:** online pre-interview with Kabel (Aleef, Widad), ~30 min, Tue 6 Oct (time TBC). Present the deck (~12 min), then Q&A.
**Answer pattern:** three sentences — the answer, the reason, one example or number.

## Opening line if they ask about the brief
"I built this from Chin Hin's brief plus public information, and the numbers are illustrative — I'd confirm them with your data."

## Likely questions and model answers

**Why this project?** It sits where my data, finance and automation work meet, and it's a real business decision, not only a technical problem.

**Why not just forecast demand better?** Forecasting doesn't decide who gets served when capacity is short, and plant data is on paper and WhatsApp. I fix the decision and the capture first; forecasting becomes realistic after a few months of clean data.

**Why would a plant manager follow a rule that hurts his own margin?** (mock Q1) The ledger records the ringgit his plant gave up for the group, so finance can credit the plant, and he has a voice in the weekly huddle. The rule also protects his margin whenever outside orders are worth more.

**Who makes the call when capacity is tight?** The scheduler applies the rule; the plant manager owns the outcome; exceptions go to a 30-minute huddle with the project director, so nothing escalates.

**How do you price internal demand without a transfer price?** Cost of delay. For property it's partly set by law: 10% a year of the purchase price per day late, so a RM300m block past its deadline is about RM82,000 a day.

**Where does AI actually help?** Four agents do the legwork — intake, matching, planning, writing. The planner calls a calculator for every number, and the scheduler approves before anything is sent. Forecasting and letting AI decide are premature.

**Isn't an agent pipeline overkill for a plant on WhatsApp?** It meets the plant where it is: orders keep arriving by WhatsApp and the agents turn them into data instead of asking staff to change tools. Each agent is small and can be switched off without breaking the board.

**What if there's little data?** Month one rebuilds recent decisions from chats and paper; that rebuild is the baseline. The logic stays the same when real data arrives.

**How will you know it worked?** Cash released from excess stock, outside margin no longer lost, project delay days avoided — not logins.

**You have no supply-chain experience.** That's why month one starts by sitting with the scheduler. My CDO role taught me to reverse-engineer how things actually work before changing them.

**How would you work with a new team?** Split into capture, matching, rule calculator/planner, and dashboard/writer; agree the data format on day one; demo every few days.

**Why Capacity & Demand over Property Booking?** It fits my agent and data skills, and the 2019 utilisation story and the new Serendah line show the stakes clearly.

## Delivery tips
- Pause after every big number (RM82,000; 2,000 m³; 22,000 m³).
- Say "illustrative" once on slides 4 and 9.
- Look at the camera, not the slide.
- Short on time: compress slides 6 and 11 to one sentence each.
- Test the Meet link, camera and mic beforehand.

## What not to say
- "AI will optimise your supply chain."
- "Head office should set a policy."
- "AI cuts forecast errors by 30–50%."
- "We'll measure adoption and logins."
- "The plant managers are doing it wrong."
- Illustrative numbers presented as Chin Hin's.
- Overselling Arcana / knowledge graphs as the core.
